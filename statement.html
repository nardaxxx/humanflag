Statement to the CCW GGE on LAWS

Second Session of the 2026 Group of Governmental Experts on Emerging Technologies in the Area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems — Geneva, 31 August – 4 September 2026

Human Flag Association

Mr. Chairman, distinguished delegates.

We are entering a robotic Wild West. Autonomous systems are being prepared for war without the capacity to recognize a person who is hors de combat.

The prohibition is old. It is customary law, and it binds every State in this room. It contains no exception for speed.

The protection works only if the signs of that condition can reach the decision process. A raised hand. A white cloth. These signals have always existed. The law requires that surrender be recognized. But a machine does not see a surrender. It detects a signal. And the raised hand, it cannot read. To a machine, white is a number, not a code.

What has changed is the window in which a signal can be read. In your weapons reviews, that window must be measured. Where the window is narrower than the signal it must admit, the obligation is not violated — it is emptied.

No one is required to do the impossible. But impossibility created by design is not fate. It is a choice. And a choice can be examined.

Thank you.

What Human Flag asks

International humanitarian law applies to autonomous systems. States say so, and it is not in dispute.

But a person hors de combat must be recognized as such, and recognition requires two things: signs that can be perceived, and time in which to perceive them and act. The signs have always existed — a raised hand, a laid-down weapon, a white cloth. What has not been publicly measured is the time.

The obligation remains in force. Whether it can still be performed is a question no State has publicly answered. Human Flag asks States to verify and document, in the reviews they already conduct, whether their systems preserve that time — and to test a published candidate signal as one additional means of recognition.

Human Flag Association — Bellinzona, Switzerland
