Statement to the CCW GGE on LAWS

Second Session of the 2026 Group of Governmental Experts on Emerging Technologies in the Area of Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems — Geneva, 31 August – 4 September 2026

Mr. Chairman, distinguished delegates.

The prohibition on attacking a person who has surrendered is customary law. It binds every State in this room.

But a person hors de combat must be recognized as such, and recognition requires two things: signs that can be perceived, and time in which to perceive them and act.

The signs have always existed — a raised hand, a laid-down weapon, a white cloth.

But those signs were made to be understood by human beings.

They are not, by themselves, machine-readable. To a machine, a white cloth is a number, not a code.

What has changed is the time in which that signal can still be read, and the system can still stop in response before taking irreversible action. In your weapons reviews, that time must be measured.

The obligation remains in force.

Ad impossibilia nemo tenetur. But an impossibility created by design is not fate. It is a choice — and a choice can be examined.

Thank you.

What Human Flag asks

Human Flag asks States to verify and document, in the reviews they already conduct, whether their systems preserve that time — and to test the published candidate signal as part of determining whether the obligation can be performed at machine speed.

Human Flag Association — Bellinzona, Switzerland
